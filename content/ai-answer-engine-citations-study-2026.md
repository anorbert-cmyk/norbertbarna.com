When buyers ask AI answer engines who provides AI integration, human-in-the-loop AI or a business website, the answers cite a very wide field of small sites and agree on almost none of them. In 180 answers from five engines to 36 buyer questions in English, Hungarian and German, I counted 1,598 citations from 1,001 domains, and 819 of those domains (81.8%) appeared in one answer only. Directories and marketplaces are the one group that returns: 60 of the 180 answers (33.3%) cite at least one.

I ran this measurement for my own site, and my site is part of the sample. It was cited in none of the 180 answers. The raw data is public and linked at the end.

## In short

- **The field is fragmented.** 819 of 1,001 cited domains (81.8%) appear in exactly one of 180 answers. Only 19 domains appear in five or more.
- **Engines rarely agree on a source.** Of 1,185 question and domain pairs, 1,058 (89.3%) were cited by one engine only, and 31 (2.6%) by three or more. One domain was cited by all five engines for the same question.
- **Directories are the repeat sources.** 209 of 1,598 citations (13.1%) go to directories and marketplaces, and 60 of 180 answers (33.3%) cite at least one. In English it is 30 of 60 answers, in German 19 of 60, in Hungarian 11 of 60.
- **Gemini mostly gives no source.** 26 of its 36 answers carried no link. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude gave sources in all 36 answers each, Google AI Mode in 30 of 36.
- **Reddit and YouTube barely appear.** Reddit: 3 of 1,598 citations, in 2 of 180 answers. YouTube: 15 citations, in 10 answers. LinkedIn: 25 citations, in 17 answers.

## Method

The measurement ran on 27 and 28 November 2026 and has three rounds. The numbers in this article come from the third round unless a sentence says otherwise.

- **Round 1, 27 November:** 90 fixed questions (30 each in Hungarian, English and German) on Google AI Mode, ChatGPT and Perplexity: 270 answers.
- **Round 2, 28 November:** the same questions on Gemini (all 90) and on Claude Haiku with web search (78, without the 12 questions about my own site): 168 answers.
- **Round 3, 28 November, the buyer round:** 36 new questions (12 per language) on all five engines: 180 answers, all usable.

The engines were ChatGPT (signed out, web search on), Perplexity (the sonar-pro model in the buyer round), Google AI Mode, Gemini (consumer app, signed out) and Claude Haiku 4.5 with forced web search. ChatGPT and Gemini are consumer app answers, Google AI Mode is a scraped result page, Perplexity and Claude are model APIs. The country was set by language: Hungary, the United States and Germany. Claude does not accept Hungary as a search country, so its Hungarian questions ran without one.

The 36 buyer questions use the words a buyer uses: “best X”, “X or Y”, “alternatives to X”, and concrete situations such as “we are a 40 person accounting firm, who can introduce AI where a person approves every decision?”. They cover AI consulting and automation, human-in-the-loop AI, MVP development for regulated products, product design, custom web applications and accessible websites.

A citation is a URL that an answer listed as a source. Each URL counts once per answer, with text fragments removed. A domain is the host name without “www”, and the country versions of LinkedIn are merged. Source types are assigned by fixed lists of domain names: 68 directory and marketplace domains, a short list of news and trade media, and patterns for EU, government and university hosts. Everything not on a list falls into “all other sites”, which is mostly provider and agency sites and their blogs, so the directory and media figures are lower bounds.

## Which kinds of sites do answer engines cite for buyer questions?

Three of four citations go to sites that are on none of my lists: providers, agencies, studios, freelancers and their blogs. Directories and marketplaces are the largest named group.

The directories and marketplaces cited most often, by number of answers: aiagencies.eu (9), arc.dev (8), clutch.co (8), toptal.com (7), behance.net, contra.com, upwork.com and themanifest.com (5 each), fratch.io, freelance.de and ai-atlasz.hu (4 each).

List pages are a second pattern that cuts across the types. 295 of 1,598 cited URLs (18.5%) have a list word in their path, such as “best”, “top”, “companies”, “agencies” or “alternatives” and their Hungarian and German equivalents. Many of these are lists published by a provider that is on its own list.

The most cited domains overall, by number of answers out of 180: linkedin.com (17), s-pro.io (11), youtube.com (10), aiagencies.eu (9), arc.dev, clutch.co and socialpro.hu (8 each), toptal.com and leventech.hu (7 each), kindgeek.com, speedmvps.com, apexweb.hu and aiworks.hu (6 each). The top domain reaches 9.4% of answers. No domain comes close to owning a topic.

The question type changes the picture. Across all three rounds, a directory was cited in 87 of 280 answers (31.1%) to questions that ask who provides something, and in 5 of 290 answers (1.7%) to informational questions. EU and government sites show the opposite: 68 of 290 informational answers (23.4%) against 18 of 280 “who” answers (6.4%).

## How often do the engines agree on a source?

Rarely. For every question I listed each domain that any engine cited, which gives 1,185 question and domain pairs. 1,058 of them (89.3%) were cited by a single engine, 96 (8.1%) by two, 26 (2.2%) by three, 4 by four and 1 by all five.

The one full agreement: for “Best AI consultants for fintech startups in Europe 2026”, all five engines cited aiagencies.eu. Four engines agreed on dieaiberater.de, kindgeek.com, hai.stanford.edu and socialpro.hu for one question each.

Seen per question: in 2 of 36 questions no two engines shared a single domain. In 17 the best agreement was one domain shared by two engines, in 12 by three engines, in 4 by four and in 1 by five.

## How many sources does each engine give?

The engines differ more in how many sources they give than in what they cite.

ChatGPT listed 744 of the 1,598 citations, with up to 42 in one answer. Perplexity returned exactly 10 in every answer. Claude gave 4 to 5 on average and never more than 7.

Gemini is the outlier: 26 of 36 answers had no link at all. Across all three rounds it gave no source in 77 of 114 answers to questions that were not about my own site. Where an engine answers without sources, a page cannot be cited, only known.

## Does the language of the question change the sources?

Yes, mostly through directories and local domains.

- **Hungarian** (60 answers, 516 citations): 282 citations (54.7%) point to a .hu domain. Directories play a small part: 11 of 60 answers cite one. The most cited domains are Hungarian providers: socialpro.hu (8 answers), leventech.hu (7), apexweb.hu and aiworks.hu (6 each), autiq.hu and automating.hu (5 each).
- **English** (60 answers, 573 citations): half the answers, 30 of 60, cite a directory or marketplace. The most cited domains are aiagencies.eu and s-pro.io (8 answers each), linkedin.com (7), arc.dev (6), youtube.com and toptal.com (5 each).
- **German** (60 answers, 509 citations): 230 citations (45.2%) point to a .de, .at or .ch domain, and 19 of 60 answers cite a directory. No domain is cited in more than 5 of the 60 answers: linkedin.com (5), then modular-ops.de, dieaiberater.de, ai-train.de, synapse-ki-beratung.de, youtube.com, fratch.io and freelance.de (4 each).

The number of sources per answer is close in all three languages: 8.6 in Hungarian, 9.6 in English, 8.5 in German.

## What do the most cited small-provider pages have in common?

After the first two rounds I fetched the 17 pages from small providers and directories that were cited most often and read what is on them. This is a description of 17 pages, not proof of cause, and it says nothing about links, domain age or indexing.

- **A short answer block at the top:** 10 of 17 pages open with a self-contained answer, often labelled (“TL;DR”, “Key takeaways”, “The short answer”, “Röviden”).
- **Quantities:** 11 of 17 state numbers where the question asks for one: durations, counts or figures about the firms they describe.
- **Tables with values:** 8 of 17 have at least one table whose cells hold values, not adjectives.
- **Named providers:** 5 of 17 are lists of named firms in a uniform format. 4 of those 5 include the publisher itself.
- **A visible date:** 9 of 17 carry the year in the title or main heading, and 9 of 17 show a publication date, an “updated” line or an “as of” line.
- **A title that matches the buyer’s words:** 5 of 17 are pages whose URL and main heading are exactly the service asked about, and two of those were cited with very little text on the page.
- **A question and answer block:** 9 of 17.

Length did not separate the pages. They range from under 500 words to about 15,500.

## What does this mean for a small provider?

These are observations from one measurement, not a recipe, and nothing here makes a citation certain.

1. **Fragmentation leaves room.** When 81.8% of cited domains appear once and the leader sits in 9.4% of answers, no incumbent holds the field. A page that is indexed and answers the buyer’s question in the buyer’s words is a candidate.
2. **“Who” questions are answered from lists.** Engines cannot name a provider from a page that names nobody. The cited pages are directories and provider overviews with names on them. I wrote one such overview after seeing this: [who builds human-in-the-loop AI in Europe](/writing/human-in-the-loop-ai-providers-europe), with my own name on it and that fact stated at the top.
3. **Directories carry a third of the answers.** A complete profile in the two or three directories that the engines cite for your service and language is a separate route into an answer. Which directories those are differs by language, as the lists above show.
4. **Give the engine something to lift.** A labelled short answer, a table with values, a date, and a sentence that says who you are, where, for whom and in which languages.
5. **Local language pays off in small markets.** In Hungarian, more than half the citations go to .hu domains and directories play a small part, so a Hungarian page competes with other small providers, not with global marketplaces.
6. **Do not optimise for one engine.** With 89.3% of sources cited by a single engine, being found by one engine says little about the others. Measure several.

If you are on the buying side, the same data is a reason to check what an answer engine tells you: it draws on a different handful of sites every time. The questions I would put to any provider are in [what to prepare before hiring an AI integration partner](/writing/before-hiring-an-ai-integration-partner).

## Limits

- **One day per round.** Answers are generated per request, and the same question can return different sources an hour later. Read totals and groups, not single cells.
- **A small sample.** 36 buyer questions, 12 per language, chosen by me around the services I offer. Other sectors may look different.
- **The engines change.** Models, search back ends and the number of sources shown are updated without notice. These figures describe 27 and 28 November 2026.
- **Different access routes.** Two engines were measured as consumer apps, one as a scraped result page and two as APIs. An API answer is not necessarily what a signed-in user sees.
- **Classification by lists.** Source types come from lists of domain names. A directory that is not on my list counts as “other”.
- **The author has an interest.** I sell AI integration and web development, and my site is in the sample. In all three rounds it was cited in 1 of 570 answers to questions that were not about it, and in 37 of 48 answers to questions about me or the site.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which sites do AI answer engines cite when buyers ask for a provider?

Mostly small provider and agency sites, and each one rarely. In 180 answers to 36 buyer questions, 1,598 citations came from 1,001 domains, and 81.8% of those domains appeared in one answer only. Directories and marketplaces were the largest named group, with 13.1% of citations and at least one citation in 60 of 180 answers.

### Do ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini and Claude cite the same sources?

Seldom. Of 1,185 question and domain pairs, 89.3% were cited by one engine only and 2.6% by three or more. In one of 36 questions all five engines cited the same domain.

### How many sources does an AI answer contain?

It depends on the engine. In this measurement the median was 21 for ChatGPT, 10 for Perplexity, 9.5 for Google AI Mode and 4 for Claude Haiku. Gemini gave no source in 26 of 36 answers.

Yes. The questions, the cited URLs per answer and the scores are in the public repository linked in the Data section. Reuse is free with a link back to this article. The measurement was run by the author for his own site on 27 and 28 November 2026.
