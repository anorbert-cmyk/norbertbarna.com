A website redesign is enough when the foundation holds and the problems sit in the look, the content, single pages or a few identifiable speed issues. A full rebuild is justified when the problem is in the foundation: the technology, the structure of the site, or what the site says about your company. My rule of thumb: list everything that bothers you, and if three items can only be solved with a different system, a different structure or a different positioning, rebuild. If every item belongs to one page or one piece of copy, redesign.

I work with companies across the EU, in English or Hungarian.

## What is the difference between a website redesign and a rebuild?

A website redesign keeps the foundation and changes what visitors see: typography, colours, layouts, copy, page templates. The content management system, the URL structure and the integrations stay.

A website rebuild replaces the foundation. That can mean a new content management system, a new page structure, new URLs, or all of them. A rebuild is a migration project with a design project inside it, and the migration carries the risk.

One slow page, one broken form or one outdated service description is neither: it is a repair.

## Six signs your website needs a full rebuild

1. **The site no longer says what you do today.** The website describes the business of five years ago. When the structure of the site no longer follows the structure of the business, new copy does not help: navigation and message have to be rethought together.
2. **Nobody dares to touch it.** The system is out of date, extensions are no longer maintained, updates break features. Every change needs the one person who still understands the setup, which is a technical and a security risk at once.
3. **The site is hard work on a phone.** Tiny text, a menu that does not open, a form that cannot be completed. Your analytics shows what share of your visits comes from phones.
4. **The slowness comes from the system.** One oversized image can be replaced. When theme, extensions and page builder together make every page slow, optimisation only treats the surface. The Core Web Vitals report in Google Search Console shows whether the problem is a few URLs or the whole site.
5. **Enquiries arrive, but the wrong ones.** The site attracts people who do not fit your offer. That is usually a positioning problem, and positioning lives in the structure and tone of the whole site.
6. **Legal requirements cannot be retrofitted.** Accessibility, privacy, consent management: if the old system can only meet them with patches, a rebuild is the moment to put them into the building blocks. This is a product designer’s view, not legal advice: which rules apply to you is a question for a lawyer.

## When is a website redesign enough?

A website redesign is enough in these cases:

- **The technology is current and maintained**, and only the content is out of date.
- **Single pages perform badly**: a landing page, the contact form, the services overview.
- **Speed suffers from a few identifiable causes**: images, one script, a font.
- **The design has aged, but structure and message are right.** Typography, colours and spacing can be refreshed on the existing foundation.
- **Your team can publish without a developer** and is happy with the editing tool.
- **The site ranks well, and you do not want to put that at risk.**

The last point matters more than it sounds. A rebuild changes URLs, templates and content at the same time, and each of those can cost rankings. If your site is found easily today, a rebuild needs a compelling reason.

## How do I rebuild a website without losing SEO?

1. **Make a URL inventory.** Record every existing address with its visits and rankings before anything is built, from Search Console and a crawler such as Screaming Frog.
2. **Decide on content, do not delete it.** Every old page gets a decision: keep, merge, redirect or give up on purpose.
3. **Plan the redirects.** Every address that changes redirects with status 301 to its new equivalent, not in bulk to the home page.
4. **Carry over titles, descriptions and headings.** What ranks today ranks for a reason. The copy may improve, the core terms stay.
5. **Build on a protected staging environment.** Search engines must not read the new site before launch, and the block must come off at launch.
6. **Check the technical layer before going live.** Sitemap, robots.txt, structured data, hreflang, load time on a phone, accessibility, consent banner.
7. **Measure on launch day.** Check Search Console for errors, test redirects by sample, verify analytics and forms.
8. **Watch for four weeks.** Rankings move for some weeks after a rebuild, and gaps that remain get targeted work.

## What does the cost of a rebuild depend on?

The cost of a website rebuild depends on six factors:

- **Pages and content**: the number and kinds of pages, and how much copy has to be rewritten.
- **A system change**: whether data has to be migrated to a new content management system.
- **Features**: shop, booking, member area, connections to other systems.
- **Languages**: every language version is its own set of pages.
- **Accessibility and privacy requirements.**
- **Maintenance**: who looks after the content after launch.

I do not name a fixed price, because every project is custom work, and the price follows from the assessment of the existing site. What belongs in a quote is covered in [How much does a website cost?](/writing/how-much-does-a-business-website-cost).

## Where to start

A website redesign or a full rebuild is not decided by how the site looks, but by whether the foundation can carry what you want to put on it. Problems in single pages and copy call for repair and redesign. Problems in the system, the structure or the positioning call for a rebuild, after measuring and with redirects planned in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I redesign my website or start from scratch?

Redesign if the platform is maintained, your team can edit it, and the problems are visual or limited to single pages. Start from scratch if the system is out of date, the structure no longer matches your business, or every page is slow because of the system.

### How long does a website rebuild take?

From the assessment to launch, a website rebuild takes a few weeks to a few months, depending on scope. In my experience content takes longest, not technology.

### Will I lose my Google rankings if I rebuild my website?

Not if you carry over the URL inventory, the redirects and the core terms. Rankings are lost when addresses change without redirects, when ranking content is deleted, or when the staging environment gets indexed.

### Should I change my CMS when I rebuild?

Only if the old system is the reason for the rebuild. A system change for its own sake brings migration work without a benefit.

### Do I need a new domain for a new website?

Keep the domain whenever possible. A domain change is a separate project with its own visibility risk, justified only by a compelling reason such as a new company name.
