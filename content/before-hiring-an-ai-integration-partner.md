Before you hire an AI integration partner, prepare five things: one workflow described by the people who do it, a baseline of how it performs today, the data and systems the AI may touch, the decisions a person must keep, and the measure that would make a pilot worth continuing. With those, a first conversation produces a scoped proposal instead of a demo.

This article is the preparation checklist I wish every company brought to the first call, the questions worth asking any partner, and the red flags I would watch for. The tone is practical; the pitch, such as it is, comes at the end.

## Why preparation changes the quality of proposals

Without a workflow and a baseline, a partner can only show you what a model can do, and every partner's model can do roughly the same things. With them, the partner has to say what will change in your work, how, and how you will know. That is a different conversation, and it separates people who integrate AI into real operations from people who sell demos. This is an editorial view from my own projects, not a market figure.

## The preparation checklist

### 1. One workflow, described by the people who do it

Pick one repeated task and have the people who do it describe the inputs, the hand-offs, the decisions they make again and again, and what a mistake costs. This mirrors the first step of my own method: map the work before the solution.

Be ready for the honest answer that the task needs simpler rules or a clearer interface rather than AI. A good partner says so in the first meeting.

### 2. A baseline

Time per case, error rate, waiting time, from your own records. If you have none, that is the first task, and it is worth doing before any partner is hired, because without it nobody can tell you later whether the pilot helped.

### 3. Data and access

Which systems hold the data, which data is approved for use, where information may go, and who approves access. Do not send passwords, customer data or confidential material to anyone before a sharing method is agreed. An anonymised one-page description of the workflow is enough for a first conversation.

### 4. What stays human

What may the AI suggest, what must a person check, and what happens on a missing or wrong answer. At Instructure, where I led end-to-end feature design for AI features in an enterprise learning platform, we started with what must stay human, not with what could be automated. The interface patterns that came out of that are in [Human-in-the-loop AI for regulated products](/writing/human-in-the-loop-ai).

### 5. A success measure and an owner

Which measure decides whether the pilot continues: task completion, review effort, output quality, response time or running cost, compared with your baseline. And one named person on your side who can pause the pilot or roll it back. In my method that owner exists from the pilot's first day.

### 6. Your constraints

Languages, regulated data, accessibility requirements, procurement rules, and your exposure to the EU AI Act. For orientation: the Article 50 transparency duties apply from 2 August 2026, and after the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) the Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 (sources: [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/), [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/eu-digital-omnibus-ai-enters-force), accessed 2026-10-21). Not legal advice; which category your system falls into is a question for your counsel.

## Questions to ask an AI integration partner

1. What would you test before building anything?
2. How do you evaluate output quality on our own examples?
3. What happens in the interface when the output is wrong, empty or late?
4. Who on your side builds, and who on ours owns it after hand-over?
5. How do we stop or roll back the pilot?
6. What will you not do? Ask for a clear “not in scope” list.
7. Which of your past projects is closest to ours, and what exactly was your role in it?
8. How do you handle our data during the prototype?

Question seven is the one most portfolios answer vaguely. Mine states role and boundaries on every case page, including what was not shipped, because that is the information you need to compare partners.

## Red flags

These are opinions from experience, not rules.

- A demo before any question about your workflow.
- No answer on failure states.
- Success promised before a baseline exists.
- No named owner after hand-over.
- A proposal that names a model before it names your task.

I believe in quality over volume. ## What a good first conversation produces

A bounded problem. A draft of access and review rules. A decision on whether to prototype. And a statement of what the prototype must prove before anyone builds. Those are the deliverables of the first three steps of my [AI integration method](/ai-integration#workflow); the prototype itself comes before any development commitment.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does an AI integration partner do?

Connects AI to the workflow, data and decisions you already have, designs the review and failure paths, and builds the pilot with your systems. The work is in the integration, not in the model.

### How long does an AI integration take?

It depends on the workflow, the data and the number of systems involved. The prototype step exists to answer this before a build; I do not quote a fixed duration in advance.

### Do we need our data cleaned first?

You need to know which data is approved and where it lives. The prototype shows what quality is enough for your task, which is usually less than people fear and more than a demo needs.

### Should we hire a consultant or a development company?

Ask who builds and who owns it after hand-over. Some partners do both; I design the workflow and build the pilot, including the web platform if one is needed. The [Kineticare case study](/work/kineticare) shows the build side of that work.

### Do you work with companies in Germany and the rest of the EU?

Yes. Communication is in English or Hungarian; German-speaking teams work with me in English.

### What does it cost?

Every engagement is scoped to the workflow, the data and the integrations, so the price follows from that scope. There are no fixed packages, and I give a proposal after the first conversation, not before.

## Where to start

Bring the workflow, who uses it, the systems and data involved, and what success would look like. That is enough for a [first conversation](/contact), and it is the difference between a proposal and a demo.
