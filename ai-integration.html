Who this is for

Where human in the loop AI earns its place.

AI integration pays off where a repeated task has real consequences and a person must still own the decision. I work with product and operations teams in fintech, banking, insurance, digital health and SaaS, in Hungary and across the EU. These are the most common starting points, each with a person reviewing what matters.

  • 01

    Fintech and banking teams

    Onboarding, support and operations workflows where every suggestion needs a reviewer, an audit trail and a clear path when the model is unsure. Raiffeisen and Benker gave me the regulated delivery habits this work needs.

  • 02

    Digital health and other high trust products

    Features for patients or clinicians where a wrong output has a cost. The AI drafts, flags and summarises; a qualified person approves before anything reaches the user.

  • 03

    SaaS product teams adding AI features

    Suggestions, drafts and summaries that users can edit, question and override. At Instructure I shaped explainable suggestions that learners and instructors could review, with source cues and recovery paths designed in.

  • 04

    Document and email heavy operations

    The AI reads, classifies and summarises incoming material; a colleague approves or corrects the result before anything moves on. AI workflow automation with a review step, not a black box.

  • 05

    Customer support with a human handover

    Draft answers from your own knowledge base. Sensitive or uncertain cases route to a person, and your team sends the reply.

  • 06

    Explainable recommendations and guided setup

    Recommendations that show their confidence and their reasons, and setup flows that walk a user through a complex task one step at a time. The SportsGambit MVP is the worked example.

Working together

How a useful question becomes a working product.

I work across product strategy, AI interaction design and custom software development. We can shape an AI feature inside an existing product, connect it to your team’s workflow, or build the web platform it needs.

Possible starting points include a draft-and-review flow, recommendations people can question, or guided setup for a complex task. We start with one useful task and establish whether AI is the right tool.

  1. 01

    Map the work before the solution.

    We walk through the task with the people doing it: inputs, handoffs, repeated decisions and the cost of getting it wrong. We agree what improvement would look like and whether simpler rules or a clearer interface could solve it.

    You leave with: a focused problem, an initial scope and a baseline to compare against.

  2. 02

    Define access and human control.

    We map the systems and approved data the feature needs, who can access them and where information may go. We define what AI can suggest, what a person must review, and what happens when an answer is missing or wrong.

    You leave with: a workflow, data-access boundaries and acceptance criteria agreed with your team.

  3. 03

    Test the experience before the build.

    We prototype with representative, approved examples and involve the people who will use it. The test includes uncertain outputs, unavailable data, editing and recovery. Together we decide whether the evidence supports development or the idea needs to change.

    You leave with: a testable prototype, evaluation examples and a clear build decision.

  4. 04

    Build a pilot that fits your systems.

    I design and develop the agreed product experience, working with your team on integration and ownership. The scope covers the interface, connections to existing systems, permissions, review states and failure paths. We start with a bounded pilot, with someone responsible for handling issues and able to pause or roll back a release.

    You leave with: a working pilot, defined responsibilities and a practical rollout plan.

  5. 05

    Measure, improve and hand over.

    We compare task completion, review effort, output quality, response time and running cost with the baseline, using the measures agreed for your workflow. We decide what to improve before wider use, document how the feature works and name who owns evaluation, support and future changes.

    You leave with: a pilot assessment, operating guidance and a decision on the next release.

In practice

What human in the loop means in practice.

Human in the loop is not a disclaimer at the bottom of a screen. It is a set of product decisions: what the AI may do on its own, what a person must confirm, how the interface shows uncertainty and sources, and what happens when the model is wrong or the data is missing. At Instructure that meant suggestions with visible source cues, review states and recovery paths. In the SportsGambit MVP it meant recommendations that showed their confidence and a guided setup the user could question at every step.

The same decisions are what the EU AI Act asks of the providers and deployers of higher risk systems: human oversight, transparency, logging and documented evaluation. I design for them from the start, as product requirements rather than as legal advice, so that whoever signs off on compliance has evidence to work with.

  • 01

    What you get

    Ranked use cases, a workflow with data-access boundaries and acceptance criteria, a tested prototype, a pilot integrated with your systems, an evaluation set and operating guidance, with a handover to your team.

  • 02

    What I do not promise

    Savings quoted before discovery, automation that decides important questions without a person, or any legal, data protection or AI Act compliance certification. Those questions we settle together with your legal counsel or data protection officer, and I make sure the product gives them what they need.

  • 03

    What stays with your team

    Ownership of the data, the evaluation and the decision to widen the rollout. I document how the feature works, who owns support and future changes, and how to pause or roll back a release.

Price and timeline

What determines the price and the timeline?

I do not publish a price list, because every AI project is custom work: the solution and the technology are shaped around your team and your users, and that is what sets the price. We can stop after discovery, after the prototype or after the pilot, and each stage leaves you with something you can use. These factors matter most:

  • 01

    The size of the workflow

    How many steps, people and decisions the process touches, and how often it runs.

  • 02

    The state of your data

    Tidy, accessible data turns into a reliable result faster than scattered or incomplete sources.

  • 03

    The number of integrations

    How many existing systems the feature has to connect to, such as a CRM, a mailbox or an internal database.

  • 04

    The level of review and security

    Regulated or sensitive data environments need more design, testing and documentation, and EU AI Act obligations widen the evidence you will want to keep.

  • 05

    Where we stop

    Discovery, prototype, pilot or full rollout. Discovery on its own gives you a decision about whether to go further.

Questions before a first conversation

Before we begin.

What does AI integration mean for a business?

Connecting AI to the workflow you already run: the data you hold, the tools your team uses and the decisions people make. The aim is fewer manual steps and faster decisions, with a person still reviewing what matters.

What is human in the loop AI?

An AI feature designed so that a person reviews, edits or approves the output where it matters. The interface shows why the AI suggested something, how confident it is, and what to do when it is wrong. This is how I design AI for regulated and high trust products.

Which industries do you work with?

Mostly fintech, banking, insurance, digital health and SaaS product teams in Hungary and across the EU: places where the output of an AI feature has consequences and someone has to stay accountable for it.

Which workflow should we start with?

One that is repetitive, heavy on documents or data, and has a clear measure of success: time saved, fewer errors or faster answers. Discovery is where we find and rank those candidates together.

Does this cover EU AI Act readiness?

As a design consideration, yes. Human oversight, transparency to users, logging and documented evaluation are built into the product from the first workshop. I do not give legal advice or certify compliance. Your legal counsel or data protection officer decides what applies to you, and the product is built so they can answer that question with evidence.

What happens to our data?

We only work with data you have approved for the purpose, and the second step of the work fixes who can access what and where information may go. Confidential material is shared only after we have agreed how to share it.

Do you also build the software?

Yes. Custom development takes the chosen use case from prototype to production, integrated with your systems, with clear ownership and a handover to your team. If the feature needs a website or a web application around it, see the web development page for that side of the work.

Which languages do you work in?

English and Hungarian. Meetings, documentation and handover are in these two languages, for clients in Hungary, Germany and across the EU. This page is also available in Hungarian.
