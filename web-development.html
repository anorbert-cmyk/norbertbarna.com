Working together

From the first conversation to a live product.

I have designed digital products for seventeen years, and I build what I design. Kineticare, a Hungarian digital health platform, I designed and developed from brand to launch: Next.js with server-side rendering, a Payload content system, streamed course video and Barion payments. The same process scales down to a company website and up to a customer portal.

  1. 01

    We get to know your goals and your users.

    We go through who the product is for, what they are looking for and what they need to get done. We look at your existing content, systems, domain and hosting, and we agree how we will know the new product is working.

    You leave with: a defined goal, success measures and the project scope.

  2. 02

    We design the structure, the content and the interface.

    A sitemap, a structure that matches what people search for, copy and interface. We check in a clickable design how a visitor gets from arrival to enquiry or purchase, before any development starts.

    You leave with: a sitemap, a content plan and clickable designs.

  3. 03

    We build it.

    A fast, accessible product that works well on a phone, with a content system and the integrations it needs, such as payment, email or a CRM. You try it in a staging environment before it goes live.

    You leave with: a working product you can try in a staging environment.

  4. 04

    We launch and measure.

    We set up the domain, the hosting, consent-based measurement and redirects from the old addresses, then submit the site to Google Search Console.

    You leave with: a live product, measurement in place and your search history preserved.

  5. 05

    We hand over.

    I show you how to update the content, document how the product works, and we agree who is responsible for further development and maintenance.

    You leave with: access, a user guide and an agreed form of support.

Price and timeline

What determines the price and the timeline?

I do not publish a price list, because every website and web application is custom work. The technology, the structure and the content are shaped around your users’ needs, and that is what sets the price and the time it takes. A company website and a customer portal are an order of magnitude apart. These factors matter most:

  • 01

    The number of pages and the state of the content

    Finished copy and images make for faster work than content we write together.

  • 02

    Custom features

    A member area, payment, booking or a customer portal: each needs design, development and testing.

  • 03

    Integrations

    How many existing systems the product has to connect to, such as invoicing, a newsletter tool or a CRM.

  • 04

    The number of languages

    A bilingual product, say English and Hungarian, handles every piece of content and structure twice.

  • 05

    Who updates the content, and how often

    Who publishes on the product, how often, and what kinds of content they need to manage.

Questions before a first conversation

Before we begin.

How much does a custom website or web application cost?

Every website and web application I build is custom work, so I do not quote a fixed price up front. The price depends on the number of pages, the state of your content, the custom features, the integrations and the number of languages. We agree the scope and the cost after the first conversation and before any work starts.

How long does it take to build?

It depends on the scope and on how much of the content is ready. We agree the schedule before design starts and work in stages, so you can see where the project stands while it is being built.

What is the difference between a website and a web application?

A website mainly informs and brings enquiries. In a web application the visitor gets something done: signs in, pays, applies or manages data. Many projects are a mix of the two, such as a company site with a member area.

Custom build or template?

A template launches quickly, but your brand, your products and what your visitors need rarely fit it exactly. I build custom web products where the structure, the content and the technology are shaped around your goals.

Which technology do you use?

Mostly Next.js with server-side rendering, a headless content system such as Payload, and the payment, email and CRM services you already use. I choose the stack to fit the product rather than the other way round, and I explain the choice so your team can maintain it.

Will we be able to update the content ourselves?

Yes. The product ships with a content system in which your team edits pages, articles and images without a developer, and I show you how at handover.

Can you add an AI feature to the product?

Yes, for example search, summaries or a support assistant with a person reviewing the output. The AI integration page describes how that work is done.

Which languages do you work in?

English and Hungarian. Meetings, documentation and handover are in these two languages, for clients in Hungary, Germany and across the EU. I also build bilingual websites. This page is available in Hungarian too.
