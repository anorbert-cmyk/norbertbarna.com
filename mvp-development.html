MVP development

An MVP that holds up in review.

MVPs for fintech, regtech, banking and digital health products that have to pass a compliance or security review before real users arrive.

If your product handles money, health data or identities, the minimum is larger than the startup textbook says. You can cut features, but identity verification, consent, data protection, confirmation of consequential actions and traceability have to be real on day one. I design and build that first version, and where the MVP uses AI, a person reviews what matters. Where a prototype would answer your question faster than an MVP, I will say so.

Discuss your project

Working together

From one assumption to the first real user.

I work across product strategy, interaction design and custom software development, so the person who writes the duty list with you also designs the flows and builds the product.

The order matters. The duty list comes before any design work, because it decides how much of the product is foundation and how much can stay a facade.

  1. 01

    Name the assumption and the duties.

    We agree the one assumption the MVP tests and list the rules that apply once a real user brings money, health data or an identity. Your legal counsel or compliance lead co-writes the list.

    You leave with: one measurable assumption and a duty list your counsel has seen.

  2. 02

    Separate facade from foundation.

    Everything that is neither the assumption nor a duty goes on the leave-out list or is done by hand behind the scenes. Identity, consent, confirmations, data protection and records are built for real.

    You leave with: a scope with three lists: build for real, do by hand, leave out.

  3. 03

    Design and test the critical flows.

    Onboarding, confirmation, errors and the way back, as a prototype tested with users and revised before product code is written. Where the MVP uses AI, we define what it may suggest and what a person must review.

    You leave with: a tested prototype of the critical flows and a clear build decision.

  4. 04

    Build in short loops.

    I build the agreed product with measurement from the start: where users drop off, where they hesitate, where they call support. Records of who approved what, and which version of the terms a user accepted, are complete from the first release.

    You leave with: a working MVP, documented flows and the evidence a reviewer will ask for.

  5. 05

    Launch small, then decide.

    The MVP goes to a few real users behind restricted access, with one named owner who reads the numbers. Then we decide together: extend, rebuild or stop, based on what users did.

    You leave with: a launch assessment, a handover to your team and a decision on the next release.

In practice

What you get, and what stays outside the scope.

Every step ends with something you can use, so we can stop at any point and the work up to then stays useful. I treat regulation as a set of product requirements, not as legal advice.

  • 01

    What you get

    One measurable assumption, a duty list written with your counsel, tested critical flows, a working MVP with identity, consent, confirmations and records built for real, and a handover to your team.

  • 02

    What I do not promise

    A licence, a certification or a passed audit. I do not give legal advice and I do not certify compliance. Your legal counsel, compliance officer or data protection officer decides what applies, and I build the product so that they can answer with evidence.

  • 03

    What stays with your team

    The licence or the regulated partner, the relationship with the regulator, the decision to open the product to more users, and ownership of the data. I document how the product works and who owns support and future changes.

Price and timeline

What determines the price and the timeline?

I do not publish a price list, because every MVP is custom work: the price follows once the one assumption and the duty list are settled. We can stop after the duty list, after the prototype or after the launch, and each stage leaves you with something you can use. These factors matter most:

  • 01

    The duties that apply

    Identity verification, payment processing, health data and AI labelling each add design, testing and documentation.

  • 02

    The integrations

    Whether a payment provider, an identity verification service or another third-party system has to be connected.

  • 03

    The foundation you already have

    An existing design system, component library or back end shortens the build.

  • 04

    The number of platforms

    Web, iOS and Android are separate pieces of work, and many MVPs start with one.

  • 05

    The depth of review

    A bank partner’s security review or an external audit asks for more evidence than an internal sign-off.

Questions before a first conversation

Before we begin.

What is an MVP for a regulated product?

The smallest version of a product that tests one central assumption with real users, with everything that concerns trust and permissibility built for real: identity verification, consent, data protection, confirmation of consequential actions and traceability. Features can wait. These cannot.

Who designs and builds the MVP?

I do. I am Norbert Barna, a Product VP with seventeen years in regulated, high-trust products. I design and build the first version myself, for founders and product teams in Hungary, Germany and across the EU. The Raiffeisen, Bitpanda, Benker and Kineticare case studies are the work behind it.

What must a fintech MVP include from day one?

Everything that concerns trust and permissibility: identity verification and consent, a clear confirmation for every consequential action, data protection built into the code, and complete records of who approved what. Without them the product may not go to real users at all.

Can an MVP skip KYC, AML checks or data protection?

No, not once real users bring real money, health data or identities. MVP status does not suspend the GDPR, payment rules such as PSD2 with strong customer authentication, or anti-money-laundering identity checks. What you can skip is secondary features, automation behind the scenes, scale and visual polish.

Do you guarantee that the MVP passes a compliance or security review?

No. I do not give legal advice, I do not certify compliance and I do not promise the result of an audit. I build the product so that your legal counsel, compliance officer or security reviewer finds what they ask for: complete records, clear confirmations and documented flows.

What if the MVP uses AI?

Then a person stays in the loop from the first version. Every AI output is labelled, shows its confidence or its source, and passes a review step before a consequential action. The Article 50 transparency duty of the EU AI Act applies to an MVP too. This is a product team’s reading, not legal advice. The AI integration page describes how I design these features.

How much does MVP development cost?

I do not quote a fixed price in advance, because every MVP is custom work. The price depends on the duties that apply, the integrations, the foundation you already have, the number of platforms and the depth of review. We agree scope and cost after the first conversation and before work begins.

Which languages do you work in?

English and Hungarian. Meetings, documentation and handover are in these two languages, for clients in Hungary, Germany and across the EU. This page is also available in Hungarian and German.
