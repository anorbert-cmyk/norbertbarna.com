Digital Health Platform — Design & Build

Kineticare

Kineticare is an online hand-rehabilitation platform built for two practicing physiotherapists. It turns one-on-one clinical expertise into structured video courses patients follow at home — from a free SOS relief program to full recovery journeys for carpal tunnel syndrome, wrist injuries, and tennis elbow. I designed and shipped the whole product end to end: brand identity, UX, content architecture, and the Next.js build with secure payments and streaming video.

Challenges

Kineticare had to earn the trust of people in pain — often after months of failed self-treatment — and convince them that an online program written by physiotherapists Kata Kiss and Kata Kocsis could genuinely help. Healthcare products live or die on credibility, and this one also had to sell courses, stream video, and read like a warm human practice rather than a faceless e-commerce site.

The key hurdles were:

  • Build clinical trust online: rehabilitation is intimate and skepticism about "online physiotherapy" is high. The design had to feel medical-grade and human at the same time.
  • Turn a 1:1 practice into a product: the therapists' in-person methodology had to be restructured into self-serve video courses that work without a therapist in the room.
  • Sell without pressure: patients research carefully before paying. The funnel needed a genuinely useful free entry point instead of aggressive conversion tactics.
  • Serve a broad, often non-technical audience: clear language, calm pacing, large touch targets, and a mobile experience as polished as desktop.

Design direction

The brand is built on calm authority: a deep navy paired with airy, light-filled neutrals, Tenor Sans for display type and Nunito Sans for reading comfort. Photography of hands — the very subject of the therapy — carries the visual identity, replacing generic stock imagery with something patients immediately recognize as being about them.

Every section of the page answers a real patient question in order: Can this help my problem? Who are these people? How does an online course actually work? What did it do for others like me? Reassurance over persuasion — headlines name symptoms and outcomes in plain language, and proof (credentials, patient stories, a transparent process) is layered throughout the scroll.

Designing the patient journey

The information architecture follows the recovery journey itself — acute pain, structured rehabilitation, prevention — rather than an org-chart of services. The free SOS Kézrelax micro-course is the deliberate front door: it delivers immediate relief exercises, proves the teaching quality, and moves visitors onto the email list and into paid programs without a hard sell.

Patient testimonials, an honest FAQ, and a step-by-step "how the online course works" section remove the remaining objections, while a members-only area gives paying patients a distraction-free space to follow their program.

Building the platform

I built Kineticare as well as designed it — a Next.js application with Payload CMS for content, CDN-streamed course video, Barion payments, a member login area, and GDPR-compliant consent. The build is server-rendered for SEO, ships a strict content-security policy, and keeps the editorial workflow simple enough for the therapists to publish without a developer.

The Tudástár (knowledge hub) is the organic growth engine: article pages target the long-tail symptom searches patients actually type — frozen shoulder, post-fracture wrist rehabilitation, tendonitis — and each article routes readers toward the course that addresses their problem.

Outcome

Kineticare shipped as a complete, revenue-ready digital product — brand, UX, content system, payments, and infrastructure — designed and engineered by one person and deployed to production. The redesigned platform gives two physiotherapists a scalable digital practice: their expertise now reaches patients far beyond the treatment room, packaged in a product that looks and feels worthy of their clinical standards.
