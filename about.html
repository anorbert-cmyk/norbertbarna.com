It starts with
a question.

For six years the question was the same one, asked inside enterprise security software: what does a person need to see to act with confidence when a wrong move costs something real? Balabit became Balasys and then One Identity, and through all three names the work stayed the same, making complex systems legible to the people responsible for them.

That is the ground everything since has stood on. In products where trust is the whole product, clarity is not decoration. It is safety.

The question travelled well. It turned out to be the right one for banking, for enterprise learning and now for capital markets.

02 / Perspective

A wider view.

At Raiffeisen Bank International the question met scale. I built and led a seven person in house design team and set the direction that took the banking app from 1.8 stars to 4.8 and past 300 million euros in transaction volume. The design kit and UX guidelines we established went on to serve teams in 16 countries: one standard instead of sixteen interpretations of it.

At Instructure the question met AI. I introduced AI assisted design to Catalog, the first time the company had worked that way, and in a single year the team shipped 42 features to production. Leading feature development on a product responsible for more than 52 million dollars in annual revenue, I set how its AI features were designed and built and held them to usability, accessibility and ethical standards, because an AI feature people cannot review is a liability with a nice interface.

At BlackRock the question meets the market itself. I lead product and strategy for an AI platform used by investment professionals across more than 10 trillion dollars in assets under management, running delivery end to end for fixed income trading so global desks can source, analyse and execute liquidity in one place.

Three industries, one thread: people who need to decide well, quickly, with something at stake.
