It starts with
a question.

Seventeen years in, I still work from the same question I started with. I spent six years designing enterprise security software, and that is where I learned to ask it: what does a person need to see to decide with a clear head when a wrong click has real consequences? Balabit became Balasys and then One Identity along the way, and under all three names my job stayed the same: making complicated systems understandable to the people who were accountable for them.

Everything I have done since stands on that. Where trust is the whole product, clarity is what keeps people safe. So I decided early that a confusing screen is as much a bug as a broken feature.

That question has stayed with me. It served me well in banking and in enterprise learning, and it serves me now in capital markets.

02 / Perspective

A wider view.

At Raiffeisen Bank International I met the same question at a much larger scale. I built a seven person in house design team and led it, and under my direction the bank’s mobile app went from 1.8 stars to 4.8 while the transactions flowing through it passed 300 million euros. The design kit and UX guidelines we put together there were later used by teams in 16 countries. One shared standard instead of sixteen separate interpretations.

At Instructure, AI entered the picture. In a product called Catalog I introduced AI assisted design, something nobody at the company had done before, and the team shipped 42 features in a year. We were working on a product that several million people used every day and that brought in more than 52 million dollars in annual revenue. I decided how we designed and built the AI features, and I was responsible for making them usable, accessible and ethically sound. An AI feature a person cannot check is not help. It is risk with nice packaging.

Today I work at BlackRock as a Vice President. I lead a product on which the managed portfolios add up to 15 trillion dollars. My team owns the product’s AI development and the design of its features, and in fixed income trading I run delivery from start to finish. The goal is simple: traders sitting in different parts of the world find liquidity on one screen, analyse it there, and execute the trade in the same place.

The industry has changed several times since. The person on the other side of the screen has not. Someone has to make a good decision quickly, with real stakes. That is who I build for.

What seventeen
years taught me.

I start where the work actually happens. At OnRobot the most important design decisions were made on the factory floor: we watched machine operators set up a task and saw where they guessed and where they preferred to ask a colleague. At Raiffeisen we started with interviews and analytics, and only then did anyone draw a single screen. I want to see the problem with my own eyes first, and only then form an opinion about it.

I also decide up front what is worth building at all, before time, money and trust go into it. At Raiffeisen that was a workshop with the stakeholders: rebuild the existing app or patch it? Only after that did we commit to a full redesign. At Instructure we set the ground rules on day one: a person always stays in the loop, whatever the AI produces is labelled and can be edited, and there is always a way back.

I put teams together so the work keeps moving when I step back. At Raiffeisen the team was up in five weeks, then came the OKRs, the two week sprints and the shared reviews where product, engineering, compliance and customer support sat at one table. At Instructure we reviewed every user flow with legal, compliance and data science before development, and released new features behind feature flags. That did not slow us down: the team still shipped 42 features in a year.

04 / What’s ahead

The next
chapter.

I want more of the work I have described above. AI whose output a person can check before doing anything with it. Products that hold up under strict regulation. And teams that keep shipping after the launch. Kineticare, a Hungarian digital health platform I designed and built from start to finish, shows what that looks like when I start from zero.

If you are working on something like that, I would like to hear about it.
