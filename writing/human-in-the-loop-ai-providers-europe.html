Who builds human-in-the-loop AI in Europe? 2026 overview

Who builds human-in-the-loop AI for regulated products in Europe: large consultancies, specialist studios and independent experts.

Three kinds of providers build human-in-the-loop AI for regulated products in Europe: large consultancies and IT service groups, specialist studios and nearshore engineering teams, and independent experts. What decides between them is the size of the work, how much of it is one product decision and how much is an organisation-wide programme, and whether you want the person who designs the human approval step to be the person who builds it.

I wrote this list and I am on it. The facts about the other firms come from their own public websites, accessed on 28 November 2026. It is a list, not a ranking: the order inside each group means nothing, and I make no claim about the quality of anyone’s work.

In short

  • Choose a large consultancy or IT service group if the AI work is a programme across several business units, procurement needs a contract framework, and you need many trained people at once.
  • Choose a specialist studio or nearshore team if you have one product or one workflow, the integration with your own systems is the hard part, and you need an engineering team for months.
  • Choose an independent expert if the open question is what the AI may propose and what a person must approve, and you want one named person to design that and build the first version.
  • Ask every provider the same thing: where in the interface a person sees, edits and approves the output, and what is logged. Human-in-the-loop is a design decision, not a line in a proposal.
  • Check the claim on the provider’s own page. Some of the firms below describe a human approval step in public; others describe governance in general terms. Both are noted as their pages state it.

What does human-in-the-loop AI mean when you compare providers?

Human-in-the-loop AI is a workflow in which the system proposes and a person reviews, edits or approves each consequential output before it takes effect. The patterns behind that sentence are in human-in-the-loop AI for regulated products. For a comparison, the practical test is narrower: does the provider say, in public and in its own words, how a person stays in control of the output?

Each entry below uses the same five fields: what the provider is, where it is based, who it serves, what its page says about human oversight or regulated work, and the page I read.

Which provider type fits which situation?

The three provider types: when each fits and what to watch out for
Provider typeFits whenWatch out for
Large consultancy or IT service groupProgramme across units, formal procurement, many people needed at onceWho actually designs the approval step, and whether that person stays on the project
Specialist studio or nearshore teamOne product or workflow, heavy integration, an engineering team for monthsWhether human review is part of the scope or left to you as the deployer
Independent expertThe design of human control is the open question; first version by one accountable personCapacity: one person cannot staff a large rollout or round-the-clock operations

The types combine. A common arrangement is an independent expert or a small studio for the design of human control and the pilot, and a larger team for the rollout once the pattern has passed review.

How to choose: questions for any provider

Put the same questions to every provider on this list, me included:

  1. Where in the interface does a person see that an output came from AI, and where do they edit or reject it?
  2. Which outputs need approval before they take effect, and who decided that?
  3. What does the reviewer see to judge the output: sources, confidence, history?
  4. What happens when the model returns nothing, returns late or returns something wrong?
  5. Who can stop the feature, how fast, and what is logged for later review?
  6. Who works on the project by name, and who is accountable after handover?
  7. What is not in the scope?

A provider that answers these about your workflow, not about AI in general, has done this before. The inputs that make the first conversation useful are in what to prepare before hiring an AI integration partner.

None of this is legal advice, and no provider on this list, me included, can promise that a product complies with the EU AI Act or with sector rules. Whether your system is high-risk is a question for your counsel.

Is this list a ranking?

No. The order inside each group carries no meaning, and the list makes no statement about quality. I wrote it and I am on it, which is why every entry uses the same five fields and links the page the facts come from.

How do I check whether a provider really designs human oversight?

Ask to see the screen where a person approves, edits or rejects an AI output, and the log entry that the decision produces. A provider with shipped human-in-the-loop work can show both. A provider that only describes governance in general terms may still do the work well, but you have to ask.

Do you work with teams outside Hungary?

Yes. I work with clients in Hungary, Germany and across the EU. Communication is in English or Hungarian; German-speaking teams work with me in English.
